# IPS Writing Reference Guide & Framework

This guide provides analytical standards, definitions, and methodology for preparing and reviewing an Investment Policy Statement (IPS).

---

## 1. Return & Risk Profile Determination

### 1.1 Return Objective Formulations
1. **Capital Preservation**: Priority is protecting principal in nominal terms. Suitable for short time horizons (< 3 years) or highly risk-averse investors.
2. **Current Income**: Focus on yield generation to cover ongoing living expenses or institutional distributions (e.g., 4% spending rule).
3. **Capital Appreciation**: Long-term growth of real wealth. Suitable for young professionals, endowments with perpetual horizons, or generational wealth.
4. **Total Return**: Balanced approach seeking combined dividend/interest income and capital gains.

### 1.2 Ability vs. Willingness to Take Risk
A key CFA Institute distinction:
- **Ability to Take Risk (Financial / Objective)**:
  - Factors: Long time horizon, large asset base relative to liabilities, stable non-portfolio income, low liquidity needs, flexibility to delay goals.
- **Willingness to Take Risk (Psychological / Subjective)**:
  - Factors: Investor's emotional reaction to past market drawdowns, sleep-at-night factor, self-reported risk tolerance questionnaire score.
- **Conflict Resolution**:
  - If **Ability is High** but **Willingness is Low**: The conservative stance prevails (Willingness). Do not force clients into higher risk than they can emotionally tolerate.
  - If **Ability is Low** but **Willingness is High**: The restrictive stance prevails (Ability). Advise client that the financial portfolio cannot sustain high loss risk without jeopardizing core goals.

## 2. The TTLLU Constraints Framework

### T - Time Horizon
- **Single-stage**: One constant objective and risk profile over the entire horizon (e.g., 20-year endowment).
- **Multi-stage**: Distinct phases (e.g., 8 years pre-retirement wealth accumulation followed by 25+ years post-retirement decumulation).

### T - Tax Considerations
- Account asset location: Tax-advantaged accounts (e.g., RMF, SSF, 401(k), IRA) vs. taxable brokerage accounts.
- Harvesting capital losses to offset gains.
- Dividend withholding taxes for international investments.

### L - Liquidity Requirements
- Emergency cash buffer (typically 3-12 months of living expenses).
- Known upcoming capital outflows (tuition, property purchase, tax liabilities).
- Illiquid assets constraint (maximum percentage allowed in locked-up alternative investments).

### L - Legal & Regulatory
- Fiduciary standards (Prudent Person Rule).
- Trust document provisions and jurisdiction laws.
- Offshore remittance rules and foreign exchange reporting (e.g., Bank of Thailand regulations on outward portfolio investment).

### U - Unique Circumstances
- Concentration risk: Client holds large shares of employer stock or family business equity.
- ESG / SRI: Negative screening (e.g., excluding tobacco, weapons, fossil fuels) or positive impact mandate.
- Faith-based restrictions (e.g., Shariah-compliant investing).

## 3. Rebalancing Strategies

1. **Calendar Rebalancing**: Rebalance at fixed intervals (quarterly, semi-annually, annually). Simple to implement but ignores market movement between dates.
2. **Corridor / Tolerance Band Rebalancing**: Rebalance whenever an asset class deviates beyond its allowed range (e.g., target 60%, band $\pm 5\%$, trigger at <55% or >65%).
   - Optimal corridor width: Wider for high transaction cost / less liquid asset classes; narrower for liquid assets or highly risk-sensitive portfolios.
3. **Cash-Flow Rebalancing**: Direct periodic contributions to underweight asset classes and take distributions from overweight asset classes to minimize unnecessary trading costs.
